US power outage minutes by state, 2014 to 2024

The average US electricity customer went 611.3 minutes, about 10.2 hours, without power in 2024, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration's "any method" series. That is the highest of the eleven years EIA publishes by state, and up from 342.0 minutes in 2023. On EIA's IEEE-standard series, which covers fewer utilities, the 2024 figure was 662.6 minutes (11.0 hours), also the highest since 2014.

Most of 2024's total came from major events such as hurricanes and ice storms. With major event days removed, the national figure was 126.0 minutes (2.1 hours), so about 79% of the year's outage time fell on major event days.

What the numbers mean

SAIDI (System Average Interruption Duration Index)
The total minutes of sustained interruption the average customer of a utility experiences in a year. EIA aggregates the utilities in each state.
SAIFI and CAIDI
SAIFI is the average number of sustained interruptions per customer in the year; CAIDI is the average length of one interruption in minutes (SAIDI divided by SAIFI). Both are in the 2023 and 2024 file below.
Major event days
Days whose outage time is far above a utility's normal range, identified under the IEEE 1366 standard. Storms and similar events drive them. The "without major event days" figures show routine reliability; the "all events" figures show what customers actually lived through.
"Any method" and IEEE series
EIA publishes one series from utilities that follow the IEEE 1366 standard and an "any method" series that also includes utilities calculating their indices another way. The any-method series covers more customers, which is why this page leads with it. Both are in the full CSV.
